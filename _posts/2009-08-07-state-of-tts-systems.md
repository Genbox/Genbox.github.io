I have this semi-blind friend that I used to work together with. He uses an application called
Zoomtext for reading online news, installing applications and so on. The only problem is that the
program is poor in quality - it always lags around the screen, crashes and is really heavy to dance
with. Being a geek by nature I tried to find alternatives and stumbled upon some online TTS (Text-To-Speech) systems
with some really good quality voices. One of them was
[iSpeech](https://www.ispeech.org/static/registerlogin?focus=login%3f). It is a web service based application where you
can upload
your content for free. But free does not necessarily mean easy, here is what I think about it:

1. It is online and thus you can only paste it some text and then get the speech results. There
   is no parsing of documents on the fly so you can read while you listen. This can be solved (sort
   of), read on...
2. Building an application that works together with the parsing engine of Zoomtext seems to be
   pretty easy. The problem is that each upload of a text portion will contain an advertisement for the
   service - you can of course get around that by paying a large amount of money, but paying \$99 each
   month (for 600 minutes of audio) for only the voice of a TTS system is not really an option.

So how come handicapped people have to live without the high quality voice provided by iSpeech (or
[odiogo](https://web.archive.org/web/20100104182050/http://www.odiogo.com/)) in a text parser engine like the one from
Zoomtext? We are way
behind on the field of assistance applications in my opinion and someone needs to put those two
together.
